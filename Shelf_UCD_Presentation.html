What is User-Centred Design?
  • An iterative design process where users’ goals, tasks, and context drive every decision
  • Designs are validated with real users at every stage
  • Not about aesthetics — about building the right thing before building the thing right
  • Standardised in ISO 9241-210 / ISO 13407
The UCD cycle

Users vs Stakeholders
USERS
Primary users
Use the app directly and often.
  • Readers who search for books, compare prices, log progress, get recommendations
Secondary users
Use it occasionally or through someone else.
  • Gift buyers (search once for a specific title)
  • Friends sharing reading lists
  • Support staff handling “wrong price” reports
STAKEHOLDERS
Affect or are affected by the product, even if they never open it.
  • Bookstores (local & overseas) — visibility, fair ranking
  • Marketplace APIs — rate limits, terms of service
  • App stores — guidelines, revenue share
  • Development team — maintenance, technical debt
  • Instructor / evaluator — grading criteria
  • Authors & publishers — discoverability, pricing control
  • Payment/affiliate partners — conversion tracking
User Research Methods
MethodStrengthsLimitsShelf Application
InterviewsDeep context, “why” behind behaviour, follow-up questionsSmall sample, time-consuming, interviewer bias5–8 readers: “Walk me through the last time you bought a book”
QuestionnairesLarger sample, quantifiable, remote-friendlyNo follow-up, self-report bias, shallow20+ readers: frequency, pain points, price sensitivity, drop reasons
ObservationReal behaviour, not self-reported; context visibleObserver effect, no “why”, privacy/ethicsWatch 3 users compare prices across tabs/sites (screen-record with consent)
Triangulation: use at least two methods. Interviews + questionnaires = qualitative depth + quantitative breadth.

What is a Persona?
A fictional but realistic archetype of a key user group, synthesised from research data.
GOLDEN RULE
Must be based on evidence (interviews, surveys, analytics), not stereotypes or team assumptions.
An invented persona creates false confidence.

User Story Format & INVEST Criteria
As a <role>, I want <goal>, so that <benefit>.
Story 1: Compare Prices (Local First)
As a reader, I want to search a title and see local sellers first sorted by price, so that I can buy it cheaply and quickly. Acceptance Criteria (Given / When / Then): 1. Given I am in country X and search “Crime and Punishment” When results load Then local sellers appear first, sorted by price ascending, each showing price, delivery time, and seller name. 2. Given no local seller has the book When results load Then overseas sellers appear with an “Overseas” label, delivery estimate, and total cost (price + shipping). 3. Given the search fails (network/API error) When the error occurs Then a clear message appears: “Couldn’t reach sellers. Check connection or try again.” with a retry button.
Story 2: Synced Reading Progress
As a reader, I want my progress synced across devices, so that I can continue where I stopped on any device. Acceptance Criteria (Given / When / Then): 1. Given I read to page 100 on my phone When I open the app on a tablet Then it shows page 100 within 5 seconds (with “Synced ✓” indicator). 2. Given I am offline When I read 20 pages Then progress is saved locally and synced automatically on reconnect. 3. Given I read on two devices offline When both reconnect Then the later timestamp wins (last-write-wins) and a toast confirms “Progress merged from [device]”.
Story 3: Drop-Risk Warning
As a reader, I want a warning when a book is likely to be dropped, so that I do not waste money on it. Acceptance Criteria (Given / When / Then): 1. Given I have 5+ books in my history When I open a book’s detail page Then I see a drop-risk level (Low / Medium / High) with a one-line reason (e.g., “You dropped 3 of 4 long, dense books”). 2. Given fewer than 5 books in history When I open a book’s detail page Then I see “Not enough history yet — add more books to see drop risk.” 3. Given the drop risk is High When I tap “Add to library” Then a confirmation modal appears: “This book has a high drop risk for you. Add anyway?” [Cancel] [Add anyway]

Problem Statement (118 words)
“
Readers buy books from many stores, yet no single place shows who sells a title nearby, at what price, and how soon it arrives. They open multiple tabs, compare manually, and still overpay or wait weeks for overseas shipping. At the same time, they abandon books they never finish — wasting money and breaking their reading habit. Existing apps either track reading _or_ compare prices, but not both, and none warn a reader “you’ll likely drop this” based on their actual history. Students and budget-conscious readers are hit hardest: they have the least money to waste and the least time to hunt. A unified app that aggregates local-first prices, syncs progress effortlessly, and predicts drop risk from personal patterns would save money, reduce friction, and help readers build a sustainable habit.
WHO
Readers, especially students and budget-conscious readers
WHAT
Fragmented comparison + no drop prediction + no unified tracking
WHY IT MATTERS
Wasted money, broken habits, time lost

Low-Fidelity vs High-Fidelity Wireframes
LOW-FIDELITY
  • Visuals: boxes, labels, placeholders — no colour or branding
  • Speed: fast and cheap to change (minutes)
  • Focus: layout, flow, structure, information architecture
  • Use: early exploration, flow testing, stakeholder alignment
  • Tools: paper, whiteboard, Figma wireframe mode, draw.io
HIGH-FIDELITY
  • Visuals: close to final — colours, typography, images, spacing
  • Speed: slower to change (hours)
  • Focus: visual hierarchy, contrast, polish, details
  • Use: usability testing (visual), dev handoff, stakeholder sign-off
  • Tools: Figma, Sketch, Adobe XD
Our approach: Lo-fi for new screens (search, book detail). Existing hi-fi (home, profile) as v1 baseline → show v2 improvements.
